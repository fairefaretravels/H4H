There are artists who make music for a moment, and then there are artists who set out to make music that outlives the moment. The Switchblades belong to the second category.

Formed in Detroit in the mid-2000s, The Switchblades—Mackinthedark (Saneca Mack), Liah Lazer (Taliah Rose), and Raquel Mohammad—created a sound that refused to stay inside one box. Hip-hop, alternative, punk, techno, soul, and electronic influences collided to create something uniquely their own.

They weren't following a blueprint. They were building one. And they were doing it independently, long before today's DIY music culture made independent distribution, social media promotion, home recording, and direct-to-fan marketing commonplace.

We set out to make classic music.Twenty years later, that's exactly what we did.
The proof

Still out there.

The Switchblades' music continues to surface across digital platforms, archives, compilations, and music databases. Their surviving NumberOneMusic profile still reflects a substantial historical audience, with thousands of plays, profile views, followers, and subscribers.

Their catalog includes:

  • Honey
  • Distant Lover
  • Sex Slave
  • Party in My Room
  • Throw It Back
  • and more

Their music traveled far beyond Detroit. The group appeared in international distribution and compilation projects, including the Urban Heartbeat series, while their music reached audiences overseas. "Party in My Room" grew from a collaboration with a UK producer/DJ who contacted the group about using vocals from "Honey."

That is what makes the group's story unusual. The music didn't disappear. It kept resurfacing.

The reunion

Today, Saneca Mack and Liah Lazer are bringing The Switchblades back to the stage. The current live lineup represents two of the group's original members, while Raquel Mohammad remains an essential part of the group's original history and songwriting legacy.

Saneca Mack×Liah Lazer
20 years later. Still classic.

This isn't an attempt to recreate the past. It's an opportunity to reintroduce the music to a new generation while giving the people who remember The Switchblades a chance to experience the songs again — a celebration of a catalog created when the group was simply trying to make something that sounded like them.

They succeeded. And twenty years later, the music is still speaking.

This isn't a throwback.This is the return.
